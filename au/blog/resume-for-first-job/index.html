How to write a resume for your first job in Australia

A resume for your first job fits on one page: contact details, a short objective, education, skills, school or volunteer roles and referees. Example inside.

A resume for your first job should fit on one page and show four things: how to contact you, when you can work, what you have done at school or outside it, and who can vouch for you. You do not need work experience to write a good one. You need to show that you are reliable and keen to learn.

Managers hiring for a casual role at a supermarket, cafe or retail store know you are starting out. They are not comparing you with someone who has five years of experience. They want to know you will turn up, be polite with customers and follow instructions.

What should I put on my first resume?

Use these sections, in this order. The examples are illustrations only: replace them with what is true for you.

Contact detailsName, mobile, a sensible email address, suburb and stateMia Nguyen, 04xx xxx xxx, mia.nguyen@email.com, Penrith NSW
Career objectiveTwo lines: the job you want and what you offerYear 11 student looking for a casual role in retail. Friendly, punctual and comfortable with cash handling from school canteen duty.

What if I have no work experience at all?

Almost everyone has more than they think. Look at what you did in the last two years and ask: did someone rely on me? Did I handle money, people or a deadline?

  • School roles: class captain, SRC, peer tutor, canteen helper, library monitor.
  • Sport and clubs: team captain, junior coach, regular training (it shows commitment).
  • Volunteering: a sausage sizzle, a charity op shop, a community event.
  • Informal work: babysitting, mowing lawns, helping in a family business.
  • Certificates: first aid, a Barista course, an RSA if you have completed it. Only list what you actually hold. Applying at a cafe? See our barista resume example.

Before Helped at canteen.

After Served students at the school canteen every Tuesday lunch for a year, handled cash and restocked the fridges before the bell.

The second version uses the same experience, described with an action and a detail. That is all a hiring manager needs.

Do I need referees on my first resume?

Australian employers often call referees, even for casual jobs, so it is worth lining up two. A teacher, a sports coach or a volunteer coordinator works well. Do not use family members. Always ask permission first and tell your referee which job you applied for. Our guide on referees on a resume explains how to list them.

Should I put my tax file number or date of birth on my resume?

No to both. Your tax file number (TFN) goes on a Tax file number declaration that you complete once you are hired, not on a resume. The ATO explains that if you do not provide a TFN (and have not applied for one or claimed an exemption), your employer must withhold tax at the top rate. So apply for your TFN before you start.

Leave off your date of birth, a photo and your address in full. Your suburb is enough.

Will I be paid less because I am young?

Possibly, and it is worth knowing before you apply. The Fair Work Ombudsman defines a junior as an employee under 21. Juniors are paid a percentage of the adult rate set by their award or agreement, and it usually goes up on each birthday.

Some rules are changing. From 1 December 2026, under the Retail, Fast Food and Pharmacy awards, employees aged 18 to 20 start moving gradually towards adult rates, with junior rates for those ages phased out by 1 July 2029. Fair Work says there are no changes for under 18s. Check the Fair Work announcement for the details that apply to you.

What mistakes should I avoid on a first resume?

  • An email address you made at 12. Create a simple one with your name.
  • Leaving out your availability. For casual roles, it is often the first thing a manager checks.
  • Two pages of padding. One clear page beats two thin ones.
  • Spelling mistakes. Read it aloud, then ask someone else to read it.
  • Copying a friend's resume. Managers in the same suburb notice.

My advice: print your resume and drop it in by hand at local shops and cafes as well as applying online, at a quiet time of day, and ask for the manager. It still works for casual jobs, and it shows exactly the confidence they are hiring for. Add a short cover letter when applying online: see how to write a cover letter for Australian employers.

First job resume questions

How long should my first resume be?

One page. You do not have enough history to fill two, and a short, clear page is easier for a busy manager to read.

Do I need a career objective?

On a first resume, yes. Two lines saying what kind of job you want and what you offer help the reader understand you quickly.

Can I list my school results?

Yes, if they are good or relevant to the job, such as maths for a role with cash handling. Otherwise listing the school and expected completion year is enough.
