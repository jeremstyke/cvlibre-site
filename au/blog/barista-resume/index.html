A good barista resume is one page that proves you can make consistent coffee, keep up in a rush and stay friendly while doing it. Put your availability, any coffee training or cafe shifts, and a few specific skills like milk texturing near the top, because a cafe owner skims it in about the time it takes to pull a shot.

You do not need years behind the machine. Cafes can train you on their blend; they cannot train reliability, so show that too.

What does a good barista resume look like?

Here is the order I recommend for Australian cafe jobs.

  1. Name and contact details: mobile, email, suburb and state. No full street address, no photo, no date of birth.
  2. Headline: one line, such as "Barista | Latte art | Available weekends".
  3. Summary or career objective: two or three lines.
  4. Availability: days and hours, plus whether you can do early starts.
  5. Skills: six to eight, mostly coffee and service.
  6. Experience: cafe roles first, then other customer-facing jobs.
  7. Certificates and training: barista course, RSA, food safety, first aid.
  8. Referees: two names, or "Available on request".

How do I write a barista resume with no experience?

Lead with what transfers. If you have worked a register, served customers, cleaned to a standard or kept calm during a lunch rush anywhere, that counts.

Three things make an entry level barista resume stand out:

  • A short coffee course. Many TAFEs and private training providers teach the nationally recognised unit SITHFAB025 Prepare and serve espresso coffee. More on that below.
  • Clear availability. Cafes run on early mornings and weekends. If you can open at 6 am on Saturdays, say so.
  • Proof you turn up. A volunteer role, a sports team you trained with every week, a school job you held for a year.

What if I have only made coffee at home?

Be honest. Home coffee is not cafe experience, and a trial shift will show it. Still, it deserves a line.

Before Coffee lover with lots of barista experience.

After Make espresso daily on a home machine; practise dialling in grind size for new beans and texturing milk for flat whites. Keen to learn a commercial setup.

What should a barista resume summary or objective say?

Use a career objective if you are new, a summary if you have cafe experience. Either way, two or three lines and no clichés. These examples are fictional: change every detail to fit you.

No experience (career objective)

Before Hardworking and passionate individual seeking an opportunity to grow.

After Friendly uni student with retail checkout experience and a completed espresso coffee course, looking for a casual barista role. Available Saturday and Sunday from 6 am and weekday mornings.

Experienced (summary)

Before Experienced barista with great skills.

After Barista with three years in busy Melbourne cafes, comfortable running a two-group machine solo through the morning rush. Calibrates grinders at opening, pours consistent latte art and trains new staff on milk texturing.

Headline example: "Entry level barista | Espresso course completed | Weekend availability".

Which barista skills should I list?

List skills a manager can test at a trial shift. "Passionate about coffee" is not one of them. Pick the ones that are true for you:

What does a full experience section look like?

This is a fictional example to show the level of detail that works. Do not copy the tasks if you did not do them.

Barista (casual), The Corner Grind, Fremantle WA, March 2024 to present

  • Prepare espresso drinks on a two-group machine during weekend peak periods, often as the only barista on the coffee station.
  • Calibrate the grinder each morning and taste-test shots before opening.
  • Texture dairy and plant-based milks and pour latte art for dine-in orders.
  • Take orders and payments on the POS system and keep the takeaway queue moving.
  • Clean and backflush the machine at close, wipe down the bench and restock cups, lids and milk.
  • Follow food safety procedures, including fridge temperature checks and allergen questions.

Notice there are no made-up numbers. A concrete task beats an invented statistic.

Which barista qualifications matter in Australia?

You can land a cafe job without a certificate, but these can tip a manager your way, and some become required depending on the venue. Rules vary, so check your state before you pay for a course.

  • Barista course: the unit SITHFAB025 Prepare and serve espresso coffee on training.gov.au covers organising the coffee workstation, selecting and grinding beans, extracting espresso, texturing milk, serving and cleaning equipment.
  • RSA (Responsible Service of Alcohol): only needed if the cafe serves alcohol. Rules are set by each state and territory. In NSW, for example, the NSW Government says RSA training is mandatory for people who sell, serve or supply alcohol, and an interstate RSA may need a bridging course.
  • Food safety: under the national Food Standards Code, Standard 3.2.2A, many food businesses including cafes must make sure food handlers are trained or have the right skills and knowledge, and must have a food safety supervisor. Food Standards Australia New Zealand explains that a formal course is one option, not the only one.

What mistakes should I avoid?

  • Calling yourself a "head barista" after six months at a quiet kiosk. A trial shift will reveal your real level.
  • Leaving out availability. For casual cafe roles it is often the deciding factor.
  • Forgetting referees. Australian cafe owners do call them; see how to list referees.

Any tips for students, backpackers or career changers?

Students: put your timetable-friendly hours in the availability line and mention any hospitality subjects or school canteen shifts.

Working holiday makers: state your visa type and how long you can stay in one place, because cafes want to know you will be around once they have trained you.

Career changers: lean on the transferable parts. Retail and call centres build speed and patience.

My advice: drop your resume in person at a quiet time, mid-afternoon rather than 8 am, and ask for the manager. Then offer a trial. Coffee is practical: let them taste it.

Barista resume questions

Do I need a barista certificate to get a job?

No. Many cafes train new staff themselves. A short course such as SITHFAB025 helps you stand out, especially with no experience, but a strong trial shift matters more.

Should I list latte art on my resume?

Yes, if you can pour it on demand. Say what you can do, such as hearts and rosettas, so the manager knows what to expect at your trial.

How long should a barista resume be?

One page. Cafe managers read quickly, and your skills, availability and recent cafe work should all fit comfortably.
