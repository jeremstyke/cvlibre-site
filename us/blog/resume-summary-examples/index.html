Resume summary examples (and how to write yours)

What a resume summary is, how long it should be, what to put in it, and 6 examples for different jobs and career stages that you can adapt.

The resume summary is the short paragraph under your name. It is the first thing a recruiter reads, so it should answer three questions fast: who are you, what can you do and what are you looking for?

How long should it be?

Two to four lines, around 40 to 80 words. Skip "I" at the start of every sentence and avoid buzzwords like "results-driven team player" unless you back them up with facts.

Examples

Retail: Customer-focused sales associate with 3 years in high-volume stores. Consistently beat weekly sales goals and trained 5 new hires. Looking to grow into a shift supervisor role.

Recent graduate: Marketing graduate from Ohio State University with an internship in social media analytics. Comfortable with Excel, Google Analytics and content calendars. Seeking an entry-level marketing coordinator role.

Healthcare: Certified nursing assistant with 2 years in long-term care, including dementia care and vital signs monitoring. Calm under pressure and trusted by families.

Warehouse: Forklift-certified warehouse associate with 4 years in fast-paced distribution centers. Strong safety record and experience with WMS scanners.

Career change: Former restaurant manager moving into HR, with 6 years of hiring, scheduling and staff training. Currently completing a SHRM-CP certification.

No experience: Motivated high school graduate looking for a first job in retail. Volunteer at a local food bank and member of the school basketball team.
