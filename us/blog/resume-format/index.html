Resume format 2026: which one should you use?

The best resume format in 2026 for most US job seekers is reverse chronological. Here is when to pick functional or combination, plus layout rules that work.

The best resume format in 2026 for most people in the US is still reverse chronological: your most recent job first, then the one before, with dates on every line. Pick a combination format if you are changing careers or returning to work, and use a purely functional resume only when you have no work history at all.

That is the whole answer in two sentences. The rest of this guide explains why, shows what each format looks like, and covers the layout details (length, fonts, file type) that people get wrong.

What are the three main resume formats?

CareerOneStop, the career site sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor, describes three common resume styles. Here is how they compare:

FormatHow it is organizedBest forWatch out for
Reverse chronologicalJobs listed from newest to oldest, with datesA steady work history in the same fieldGaps between jobs are easy to see
FunctionalSkills grouped by theme, few or no datesNo previous employment, skills built outside paid workMany employers view it with suspicion
Combination (hybrid)A strong skills section, then a dated work historyCareer changers, people returning after a breakCan run long if you repeat yourself

CareerOneStop is blunt about the functional style: employers tend to distrust it because it hides dates and employer names. That matches what recruiters say, and it is why I would not use it unless you truly have nothing to put in a work history.

Which resume format do recruiters prefer?

Reverse chronological, by a wide margin. A recruiter spends a few seconds on a first pass and wants to answer three questions fast: what are you doing now, what did you do before, and does it match this job? The chronological layout answers all three at the top of the page.

It also works better with applicant tracking systems, which read job titles, employers and dates from predictable places. Our guide to ATS-friendly resumes explains what those systems look for.

When should I use a combination resume instead?

Use it when your most recent job is not your best argument. Three common cases:

  • Career change: a former retail manager applying for an operations coordinator role can open with scheduling, budgeting and team leadership, then list the retail jobs below.
  • Returning to work: after a few years caring for family, a skills section shows what you can do today before the reader reaches the dates.
  • Varied short jobs: gig work, temp contracts or seasonal jobs make more sense grouped under the skills they built.

Keep the dated work history even in a combination resume. Dropping it is what turns a hybrid into a functional resume, and that is where you lose trust.

What order should the sections go in?

  1. Header: name, phone, professional email, city and state, LinkedIn URL if it is up to date.
  2. Summary: two or three lines aimed at this job. See our resume summary examples.
  3. Work experience (or Skills first, in a combination resume).
  4. Education, plus certifications and licenses.
  5. Skills: tools, software and languages that appear in the job posting.

Recent graduates can move education above experience. Everyone else should keep it lower, because a recruiter cares more about what you did last year than where you studied.

How long should a resume be in 2026?

One page if you have under about ten years of experience, two pages if you have more and every line earns its place. Nobody gets an interview because their resume was longer. If you are spilling onto a second page by four lines, cut the oldest bullet points rather than shrinking the font.

Federal jobs are the exception to general advice: USAJOBS announcements set their own rules for resumes, so read the "How to apply" section of each posting and follow it to the letter.

What layout details make a resume look professional?

  • A clean, readable font such as Calibri, Arial or Georgia, around 10.5 to 12 points for body text.
  • Margins of roughly half an inch to one inch, and the same spacing between every section.
  • Bullet points that start with an action verb and include a number when you have a real one.
  • No photo, date of birth, marital status or Social Security number. US resumes leave personal details off.
  • Save as PDF unless the posting asks for a Word file, and name it clearly: Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf.

Before Responsible for customer complaints.

After Resolved about 30 customer complaints a week by phone and email, and wrote a FAQ that the whole team now uses.

The numbers in that example are placeholders. Use your own, and if you do not have an exact figure, describe the scope honestly instead of inventing one.

What are the most common resume format mistakes?

  • Two-column templates with icons and skill bars that ATS software cannot read.
  • Mixing date styles (05/2024 in one line, May 2024 in the next).
  • A functional resume used to hide one short gap, which raises more questions than the gap would.
  • Listing duties copied from the job description instead of results.

Resume format FAQ

Is a functional resume ever a good idea?

Rarely. It can help when you have no paid work history at all, but most employers prefer to see dates. A combination format usually gives you the same benefits without the suspicion.

Should my resume be one page or two?

One page for most people with under ten years of experience. Two pages are fine for longer careers if every line is relevant to the job.

Should I send my resume as a PDF or Word file?

PDF keeps your layout intact and is accepted almost everywhere. Send a Word file only when the employer asks for one.
