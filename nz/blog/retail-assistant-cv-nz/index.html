A good retail assistant CV is short, friendly and specific: one or two pages that show you can serve customers, handle money accurately and turn up for the shifts the store needs. Put your availability near the top, back up every skill with a real example, and you will already be ahead of most of the pile. Store managers want to know three things fast: can you talk to people, can they trust you with the till, and can you work when they need you.

What does a good retail assistant CV look like?

Keep the layout plain, in one column, and use this order:

  1. Name and contact details: mobile number, email address and your suburb or town.
  2. Headline: one line saying who you are and what you want.
  3. Profile: two or three sentences about what you bring.
  4. Availability: the days and times you can work.
  5. Skills: six to eight, each one you can back up.
  6. Work experience: most recent first, with real tasks.
  7. Education: school, NCEA level, any courses or certificates.
  8. Referees: two names, or a note that details are available on request.

How do you write a retail assistant CV with no experience?

Lead with what you can prove from school, sport, volunteering and home life. Employers hiring for entry-level shop roles know you are new. According to the careers.govt.nz retail sales assistant profile, there are no specific entry requirements for the job, and English and maths to at least NCEA Level 1 are described as useful. So say which NCEA level you have (or are working towards) and mention those subjects if you did well in them.

Then use anything where you dealt with people, money or responsibility: a sausage sizzle stall at a club fundraiser, volunteering at an op shop, babysitting, helping in a family business, or being a team captain. Unpaid op shop work is real retail experience, so list it under Experience. For more ideas, see our guide to writing a CV with no work experience.

Which headline and profile should you use?

Make the first lines specific to retail and to the store. A generic line wastes the best space on the page.

Before Hard-working, motivated person looking for any job.

After Year 13 student seeking a part-time retail assistant role. Friendly, reliable, and available weekends and after school.

Profile example: no experience

"Year 13 student in Hamilton with NCEA Level 2 and a strong interest in fashion. Through two years of volunteering at a local op shop, I have learned to serve customers at the counter, handle cash carefully and keep displays tidy. Available Saturdays, Sundays and weekday evenings from 4pm."

Profile example: experienced

"Retail assistant with three years in homeware and gift stores, comfortable running the till, opening and closing the shop, and training new team members. Known for calm complaint handling and keeping the floor stocked during busy sale periods. Looking for a full-time or senior sales assistant role."

What skills should a retail assistant put on a CV?

The careers.govt.nz profile lists qualities such as good communication, friendliness, customer service, honesty and reliability, plus cash handling, basic maths and knowledge of the Consumer Guarantees Act. Pick what matches the job ad and pair each skill with proof.

What does a full experience section look like?

Here is a complete example. The person and the store are fictional; replace every detail with your own.

Retail Assistant (part-time), Harbourside Outdoor Gear, Nelson
March 2024 to present

  • Greet and serve customers, advising on clothing sizes, footwear fit and camping gear for different trips
  • Process cash, EFTPOS and gift card payments, and balance the till at the end of each shift
  • Handle returns and exchanges following store policy and customers' rights under the Consumer Guarantees Act
  • Unpack deliveries, check them against packing slips and replenish shelves
  • Update window and in-store displays for each season, working to head office guidelines
  • Take part in the yearly stocktake and weekly spot counts
  • Open and close the store on weekends, including alarm, cash float and end-of-day checks

How should you show your availability?

Put it near the top, right under your profile. The careers.govt.nz profile notes that retail assistants usually work regular business hours but may also work evenings and weekends. Weekends, late nights and holidays are where stores most need people. Be precise:

Before Flexible hours.

After Available: Saturday and Sunday all day, Monday to Thursday from 3.30pm, late nights until 9pm. Full-time over the summer holidays.

My view: wide availability can matter as much as experience, so never bury it. If yours is limited, say so anyway, because being rostered for shifts you cannot do ends badly.

Which mistakes cost retail applicants interviews?

  • Sending the same CV to every store. Mention the store's products in your profile.
  • Listing skills with no proof. "Excellent customer service" needs a line showing where you did it.
  • Spelling mistakes. A typo suggests careless till work.
  • An unprofessional email address. Use one with your name.
  • Leaving out availability. Many managers look for it first.
  • Referees who do not know you are coming. Ask first. Our guide on referees on a CV in NZ explains who to choose.

Any tips for school leavers, career changers and newcomers?

Students and school leavers: one page is enough. Put education first if you have no paid work yet, and hand your CV in person when the store is quiet.

Career changers: hospitality, call centre, reception and care work all give you customer skills a store needs. Keep your job titles but rewrite the bullets around serving people and handling payments.

Newcomers to New Zealand: list overseas retail work with the country and type of store, and put any extra languages in your skills. Many shops serve customers from all over the world.

Once you are in the job, you can grow. The careers.govt.nz profile says you can work towards a qualification on the job, such as the New Zealand Certificate in Retail (Levels 3 and 4). If you are already studying for it, add it under Education.

Retail assistant CV: common questions

How long should a retail assistant CV be?

One page is ideal if you are a student or have little experience. Two pages is fine if you have several years in retail.

Do I need a qualification to become a retail assistant in NZ?

No. careers.govt.nz says there are no specific entry requirements, though English and maths to at least NCEA Level 1 are useful. You can work towards the New Zealand Certificate in Retail on the job once you are hired.

Should I include a cover letter with my retail CV?

Yes, if the ad asks for one or you apply online. Keep it to three short paragraphs: why this store, what you can offer, and when you are available.
