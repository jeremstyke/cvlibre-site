How to fill in this CV template

Replace every text in square brackets with your own details, then delete the brackets. The headings and bullet points use real Word styles, so to add another job you can copy an existing block and paste it underneath: the formatting follows.

  • Contact details: your name, town or city, phone number and email address are enough. You do not need your full postal address.
  • Leave out personal details. The National Careers Service says you should not include your age, your date of birth, whether you are married or your nationality. A photo is not expected on a UK CV either.
  • Personal statement: three to five lines at the top that sum up who you are, what you offer and the job you want. Rewrite it for each application.
  • Work history: most recent job first, with dates. Start each bullet with an action verb and add a figure when you can: customers served, targets met, money saved.
  • Education: early in your career, you can put it before your work history. List qualifications with grades, including GCSEs in English and maths if they are recent.
  • References: you do not need to give referees' contact details on your CV. "References available on request" is enough, as long as you have people ready to vouch for you.
  • Length and font: two pages at most, in a clear font such as Calibri or Arial at 11 points or bigger. The templates already use Calibri.

Which template should I choose?

The classic CV suits almost every job. The modern CV is useful when your skills matter more than your job titles, for example after a career break or a change of sector. The template with no experience is for a first job, a Saturday job, an apprenticeship or a placement: school projects, sport and volunteering all count as evidence.

The elegant and two-column designs stand out when you email your CV or hand it in. For online application portals, the classic CV is the safest choice, because some applicant tracking systems struggle with columns.

Before you send your CV

Check your spelling and grammar, or ask someone to read it for you. Unless the employer asks for a Word file, save it as a PDF (File, Save As, PDF) so the layout stays the same on their screen, and give it a clear name such as "Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf". Tailor the personal statement and the order of your skills to each job advert: it is the quickest way to stand out, and it helps with applicant tracking systems.

Frequently asked questions

Are these Word CV templates really free?

Yes. There is no account, no email and no payment: you download the .docx file and use it for your job applications.

Should I put a photo on my CV in the UK?

No. UK employers do not expect a photo, and leaving it out keeps the focus on your skills and experience. The same goes for your age and date of birth.

How long should a UK CV be?

Two pages at most. One page is fine if you are a student or have little experience. These templates fit on one page, with room to grow onto a second.
