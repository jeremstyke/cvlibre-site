A teaching assistant CV has one job: convince a head teacher or SENCO that you will be calm, reliable and useful with children from your first morning. Lead with a short personal statement, then any experience with children (paid, voluntary or family), then your qualifications and checks, and keep it to one or two pages.

Schools often shortlist from a pile of applications in a single sitting. The ones that get picked use the words from the advert, show real moments with children and make the practical details easy to find.

What goes on a teaching assistant CV, and in what order?

This is the order I would use for a school job in the UK. Every section below can be filled in directly in our free CV builder.

SectionWhat to put in it
Name and contact detailsPhone, email, town or postcode area. No photo, no date of birth
HeadlineThe job title from the advert, for example Teaching Assistant (KS1) or Learning Support Assistant
Personal statement3 or 4 lines: who you are, your experience with children, what you are looking for

Other workOlder or unrelated jobs, one or two lines each
Education and trainingGCSE English and maths, any childcare or supporting teaching qualification, first aid, safeguarding courses
Extra skillsPhonics schemes you know, Makaton, a second language, IT

Teaching assistant CV headline and personal statement examples

The headline is not a slogan. It is simply the role you are applying for, copied from the advert. If the school writes "Learning Support Assistant, 1:1 SEND", that is your headline.

Before Enthusiastic and dedicated individual seeking a rewarding role in education.

After Teaching Assistant | KS1 phonics support, small group reading, Level 3 Supporting Teaching and Learning

The personal statement sits right under your name. Three or four lines is plenty. Here are two you can adapt.

Personal statement with no school experience

"Patient and organised parent of two, returning to work after five years at home. I volunteer weekly as a reading helper at my children's primary school and run the Beavers colony in my village. I am looking for a part-time teaching assistant role in a primary school where I can support small groups with reading and early maths."

Personal statement for an experienced TA

"Teaching assistant with six years' experience in Year 1 and Year 2, including two years supporting a pupil with an EHCP on a one-to-one basis. Confident delivering phonics interventions and keeping clear progress records. Holder of a Level 3 certificate in supporting teaching and learning, now looking to develop towards HLTA."

Teaching assistant CV example: the experience section

The example below is fictional. Replace the school, dates and tasks with your own. Notice that each bullet describes something a teacher could actually watch you do, rather than a quality you claim to have.

Teaching Assistant, Oakfield Primary School (example), Bristol | September 2022 to present

  • Support a Year 2 class of 30 pupils alongside the class teacher, mainly in literacy and maths
  • Run daily phonics catch-up sessions for groups of 4 to 6 children and record their progress for the teacher
  • Work one-to-one with a pupil with autism, using a visual timetable and agreed calm-down strategies
  • Prepare resources, set up the classroom and display pupils' work
  • Supervise playtimes and lunchtimes, and look after children who are upset or unwell
  • Share daily updates with parents at pick-up when the teacher asks me to

These match the duties the National Careers Service lists for the job, so the reader recognises them at once.

No classroom experience yet? What to put instead

You do not need to have worked in a school to get your first TA job. The National Careers Service lists college courses, an apprenticeship, volunteering and applying directly to schools, local authorities and academy trusts as routes in. What a school needs to see is evidence that you are good with children and dependable.

  • Volunteering in a school: reading helper, school trips, after-school clubs. Ask your local primary, many welcome regular volunteers
  • Youth groups and sport: Scouts, Guides, Sunday school, junior football coaching
  • Childcare: babysitting, nannying, holiday play schemes, helping at a nursery
  • Parenting: a career break to raise children is not a gap to hide. Describe what you did at the school gate as a volunteer, not just at home

Put this experience straight after your personal statement under a heading such as "Experience with children". Our guide to a CV with no experience has more ideas for filling the page honestly.

Which skills do schools look for in a teaching assistant?

The National Careers Service teaching assistant profile lists sensitivity and understanding, excellent verbal communication, active listening, patience and the ability to stay calm under stress, flexibility, and the ability to create good conditions for learning. Do not paste those words as a list. Pair each one with a short proof in your experience section, then add practical skills like these:

  • Phonics delivery (name the scheme the school uses if you know it)
  • Small group and one-to-one interventions
  • Supporting pupils with SEND, including following an EHCP or support plan
  • Behaviour management using the school's policy
  • Observing and recording pupil progress
  • Paediatric first aid
  • Makaton or British Sign Language, if you have it

Qualifications and checks for a teaching assistant CV

Each school sets its own requirements, but the National Careers Service says you will usually need GCSEs at grades 9 to 4 (A* to C) in English and maths. Always put those near the top of your education section. Other qualifications that schools recognise include:

  • College courses such as supporting teaching and learning in schools, childcare and education, or the T Level in Education and Early Years
  • The Teaching assistant (level 3) apprenticeship: the government apprenticeship training site gives a typical duration of 18 months and lists it as equal to A level
  • Higher level teaching assistant (HLTA) status: the next step for experienced TAs who take on more teaching responsibility
  • Short courses: safeguarding, paediatric first aid, autism awareness, phonics training. List the year you completed each one

Working with children means you will need to pass an enhanced background check. The school organises this, so you do not need one before applying, but if you already hold an enhanced DBS certificate on the Update Service you can mention it in one line.

Mistakes that cost TA applicants an interview

  1. "I love working with children" and nothing else. Almost every applicant writes it. Show one moment where it made a difference.
  2. Naming pupils or sharing details about them. Never include a child's name, diagnosis or family situation. Safeguarding is the first thing a head will think about.
  3. Ignoring the age group. A CV for a secondary SEN role should not read like one for Reception. Adjust your examples to the key stage in the advert.
  4. A CV with no cover letter. Most schools use an application form or ask for a supporting statement. Read the advert carefully and send exactly what they ask for.

Teaching assistant CV: frequently asked questions

How long should a teaching assistant CV be?

One page is enough if you are new to schools. With several years of classroom experience, two pages is the maximum. Schools read many applications, so short and clear wins.

Can I become a teaching assistant with no qualifications?

Some schools take on people without a specific TA qualification, especially if they have volunteered. However, the National Careers Service says you will usually need GCSEs at grades 9 to 4 in English and maths, so check the advert before applying.

Should I put my DBS check on my CV?

You can add one line if you already hold an enhanced DBS certificate registered with the Update Service. If you do not have one, leave it out: the school will arrange the check before you start.
