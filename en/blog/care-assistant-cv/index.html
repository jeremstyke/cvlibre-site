Care Assistant CV: Example and Tips (UK)

How to write a care assistant CV in the UK: personal statement examples, skills, an example experience section, Care Certificate, DBS and no-experience tips.

A good care assistant CV shows two things fast: that you can be trusted with vulnerable people, and that you can handle the practical side of personal care. Put a short personal statement at the top, then your care experience (paid, voluntary or family), then your training and checks, all on one or two pages.

Care managers often read CVs between shifts. They want kindness backed by evidence and the words from their advert, whether it says care assistant, carer or healthcare assistant.

What should a care assistant CV include?

Here is the order I recommend. In care, the reader wants to know what you have done with people before anything else. Each of these sections is ready to fill in our CV builder.

Name and contact detailsPhone, email, town or postcode area

Personal statement3 to 4 lines on who you are and why care

Care assistant CV headline and personal statement examples

Your headline is simply the job title you want. Copy it from the advert. If a care home asks for a "Care Assistant (Nights)", that is your headline. If an NHS trust advertises a "Healthcare Assistant" post, use that.

Before Hardworking and passionate individual looking for a rewarding opportunity.

After Care Assistant | Personal care, dementia support, Care Certificate completed

The personal statement sits just under your name. Keep it to three or four lines and make every one count. Here are two you can adapt.

Personal statement for a beginner

"Caring and reliable school leaver looking for my first role as a care assistant. For the past two years I have helped look after my grandmother at home, including meals, medication reminders and getting to appointments. Calm under pressure, happy to work weekends and keen to start the Care Certificate."

Personal statement for an experienced carer

"Care assistant with four years' experience in residential and domiciliary care, mainly supporting people living with dementia. Confident with personal care, moving and handling equipment and digital care records. Holder of the Level 3 Diploma in Adult Care, now looking for a senior carer role in a nursing home."

Care assistant CV example: the experience section

This is a fictional, illustrative example. Swap in your own employer, dates and tasks. Notice that every bullet describes something a manager could picture you doing.

Care Assistant, Willow Court Care Home (example), Leeds | March 2023 to present

  • Support up to 8 residents per shift with washing, dressing, continence care and personal hygiene, respecting their routines and choices
  • Help residents at mealtimes, record food and fluid intake and flag concerns to the nurse in charge
  • Use hoists, slide sheets and standing aids safely, following each resident's moving and handling plan
  • Take routine observations (temperature, pulse, blood pressure) and report changes straight away
  • Update care plans and daily notes on the home's digital care system before handover
  • Spend one-to-one time with residents living with dementia, using life story work to reduce distress

Care assistant CV with no experience: what to put instead

You can get an entry level care job without paid care experience. The National Careers Service lists applying directly, volunteering, college courses and apprenticeships as routes into care work. What employers need is proof of the right values, so look for it in your own life.

  • Caring for a relative or neighbour: describe it like a job, with the tasks you did
  • Volunteering: befriending services, hospital volunteering, lunch clubs, food banks
  • Customer-facing work: retail, hospitality and call centres show patience and communication
  • Childcare or babysitting: responsibility, routines, keeping someone safe
  • Short courses: first aid, food hygiene

Put this experience straight after your personal statement and give it a heading such as "Caring and volunteering experience". Our guide to writing a CV with no experience has more ideas for filling the page honestly.

Skills and qualities care employers look for

The National Careers Service care worker profile highlights sensitivity and understanding, a desire to help people, patience, staying calm in stressful situations, good verbal communication and attention to detail. Mix those qualities with practical skills:

  • Personal care (washing, dressing, continence care)
  • Moving and handling, including hoists
  • Dementia awareness
  • Safeguarding adults
  • Infection prevention and control
  • Recording and reporting, including digital care records

Which qualifications and checks should go on a care CV?

You do not need a qualification to start as a care assistant in most settings, but anything you hold belongs on your CV. The main ones employers recognise in England are:

  • The Care Certificate: a set of standards (16 since the 2025 update, according to Skills for Care) that employers often use to induct staff who are new to care. If you have completed it, say so and give the year.
  • Level 2 Adult Social Care Certificate: a newer, Ofqual-regulated qualification described by Skills for Care as a baseline standard for care.
  • Level 3 Diploma in Adult Care: useful if you are aiming for senior carer roles.
  • Apprenticeships: the National Careers Service lists the Adult Care Worker (Level 2) and Lead Adult Care Worker (Level 3) apprenticeships.
  • DBS check: care workers who provide personal care can be asked for an Enhanced DBS check with an Adults' Barred List check, according to GOV.UK. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own checking services.
  • Right to work: every employer must check it before you start (GOV.UK). You do not need to explain your status on the CV, but have your documents or share code ready.

Mistakes that cost care applicants interviews

  1. Writing "I love helping people" and stopping there. Everyone writes it. Show one moment when you did.
  2. Using confidential details. Never name residents, patients or clients, even with good intentions. It is a red flag for any care manager.
  3. Leaving out the shifts you can work. Nights, weekends and a driving licence are real advantages. Say so.
  4. Listing training you have not finished as complete. Write "working towards" instead. It will be checked.
  5. Unexplained gaps. One honest line is enough, for example time spent caring for family.

Moving into care from another job or as a first job

Career changers often have more to offer than they think. A former hairdresser knows how to put nervous people at ease. A shop supervisor has handled complaints and rotas. Describe those strengths in care language, not in the terms of your old job.

For a first job, ask each employer what training new starters get. A home that invests in your induction is worth more at the start than a slightly better rate somewhere that leaves you alone.

Care assistant CV: frequently asked questions

How long should a care assistant CV be?

One page if you are new to care, two at most with several years of experience.

Is a carer CV different from a healthcare assistant CV?

The structure is the same. A hospital healthcare assistant CV leans on clinical tasks such as observations, while a home care CV stresses independence, time management and a driving licence. Match the advert's wording.

Should I put my DBS certificate number on my CV?

No. Simply write that you hold an Enhanced DBS check and, if it applies, that you are registered with the Update Service. Share the details when the employer asks for them.
